Product work at companies

Amazon / API product

Spider

Problem

Amazon's supply chain software was built for the United States, where sales tax is added on top of the price. In Brazil, tax is part of the price, so it is a cost to the company, and it changes from state to state. The systems were miscalculating what it cost to ship a product from each warehouse.

My role

When I joined, Spider was a project that touched a single supply chain system. I was hired to turn it into a product: an API that other systems consult to get that calculation right.

What I did

Wrote the product vision and a three-year plan, and PRFAQs for new features. Created a discovery framework and KPIs, and used the metrics to find new opportunities, such as better caching and new transfer routes. The product depended on many stakeholders, so I set up one communication mechanism per audience, from a short monthly report for leadership to weekly meetings with the direct team, aligning operations, business, tax, finance and technology.

Outcome

Spider increased Amazon Brazil's yearly profitability by USD 11MM, and its impact grew 4.5 times in one year. What I took from it: a product vision does most of the work of aligning stakeholders.

AutoNation / Internal platform

Configurable fee system

Problem

The fees charged by AutoNation's mobile service were hardcoded. Launching or changing a fee required engineering work.

My role

It started as a single task. I interviewed stakeholders and grew it into a product vision. Having worked with tax at Amazon helped me make the case to leadership.

What I did

Designed a fee model that finance configures without code. A fee can apply to a whole repair order, a repair or a component; be a fixed amount or a percentage of the total, of labor or of parts; and follow rules by state, customer type, partner and validity dates. Permissions follow roles: finance creates fees, account managers assign them to fleets, and operations adjusts them on an order. I also wrote the role-based training, the launch announcement and the demo.

Outcome

Launched in January 2025. Business teams now launch and adjust fees on their own, and fee-based revenue rose by up to 587%. On the same team I led the Mobile Service Partner API from zero to its first customer, including how quotes are exposed to partners: fully quoted, a price range, or pending.

Stefanini Manufacturing / AI product discovery

Watchtower

Problem

Watchtower was a proposed AI product for industrial maintenance. My job was discovery: find out whether the problem was real and whether the company could build a product that solved it.

My role

Product manager leading a team of five (developer, data scientist, sales, marketing and UX designer), presenting the status to C-level executives every month.

What I did

Ran nine months of discovery. Tested demand with landing pages and ads from the first week. Interviewed maintenance teams, which led us to pivot the problem we were solving. Wrote a product vision, ran a Lean Inception and designed an MVP simpler than first planned. Studied the market and the competition.

Outcome

The discovery showed that a competitive product needed an AI accuracy that depends on a volume of data the company did not have, in a market filling up with cheaper solutions. The product was discontinued before it was built, saving the cost of developing it.

AI agents / Open source

diar.ia.br Studio

My role

Founder and editor of diar.ia.br, a weekday AI newsletter in Portuguese. I designed and built the pipeline that produces it.

Product

A public production pipeline on Claude Code that takes each edition from research to scheduling in six stages, with human review at two of them. Each edition reviews about 300 articles and publishes nine, and goes out at 6 a.m., Monday to Friday.

How it works

40 specialized AI agents and 44 skills, integrated with 20+ external APIs and 8 MCP servers. Built in TypeScript on Node, with typecheck, unit tests and an end-to-end smoke test on every pull request.

Outcomes

Automated the work previously done by a team member. The newsletter reaches 150,000+ people a month across its own audience and partner audiences.

Augmented reality / Open source

Jandig

My role

I started Jandig in 2011: researched its feasibility, negotiated the first two exhibitions, and ran weekly meetings with a small group that built the app, the website and an open call for digital artworks. In 2018 I pitched it at MozFest in London, winning a Mozilla Seed Award. I still lead product, community and technical coordination.

Product

An open-source augmented reality platform for people who do not code. It runs in a standard mobile browser, with no app to install, and a first-time user takes about 12 minutes to publish a work.

Evolution

The first marker was pasted in November 2011. Low-cost phones of the time could not keep up, so the team paused the project and resumed it in 2017. The platform launched in 2019. In 2025 we began a mixed reality viewer for Meta Quest, first shown at Mozilla Festival in Barcelona. See the talks.

Outcomes

More than 7,200 active users in the 12 months to July 2026. Used in a Rosas de Ouro carnival parade in São Paulo at the invitation of USP, by more than 200 UEMG students over three years, and in a Sesc arts circuit across nine cities. Mozilla Open Source Support Award and Software Livres Culturais award (2018). Selected by Anthropic for the Claude for Open Source Program (2026).

Geojournalism / Open-source platform

Jeo

My role

InfoAmazonia already existed when I joined: I was hired as a consultant to diagnose its platform and propose improvements. From that work I conceived Jeo, made the case for building it, and led its development, later heading product and technology with a team of designers, engineers and data analysts.

Product

An open-source geojournalism platform for WordPress: news stories geo-referenced on maps. It grew out of the system behind InfoAmazonia, which aggregates news in Portuguese, Spanish and English from partners and the public.

Evolution

Before, InfoAmazonia ran on a manual Jekyll-based system that generated static content published straight to a Git repository, without even a search box. With no budget to maintain a proprietary platform long term, I proposed an open-source WordPress theme and a community around it, so that the cost of maintenance would be shared. That became Jeo.

Outcomes

InfoAmazonia's pageviews grew by up to 400% in one year. In its first year, Jeo was forked by 10 developers and gave rise to five new geojournalism platforms. I coordinated its adaptation for newsrooms on three continents, including Ekuatorial (Indonesia), LandQuest (Kenya) and Oxpeckers (South Africa); InfoNile (Nile Basin) adopted it later. It is still maintained as a WordPress plugin, and InfoAmazonia still runs on it.

Open hardware / IoT

Rede InfoAmazonia

My role

The idea was mine. I presented it to the InfoAmazonia team, led the proposal submitted to Google, planned the project, hired the team, managed hardware and software development, led the test deployment and managed the partnerships.

Product

Mãe d'Água, an open-source, modular device that measures water quality every hour (pH, ORP, conductivity and temperature) and sends the data over the mobile network. The software was designed for three audiences: an API for developers, a web interface with history for scientists, and SMS alerts for communities. The device cost under USD 2,000 when the cheapest commercial equipment for real-time monitoring cost more than USD 4,000.

Evolution

One of the 10 projects funded among 800+ applicants to Google Impact Challenge Brazil 2014. A specialist told me that giving communities a direct verdict on their water was not feasible, so I changed the scope: the sensors raise an alert, and a partner university laboratory tests the water. In late 2015, the communities themselves installed the equipment at 18 points in three cities in Pará; two more went to São Paulo.

Outcomes

The Google funding sustained the project for two years. The technology was used in research at six Brazilian public universities and institutes and in a master's thesis, and it inspired groups in Spain and China to build their own open-source water monitors. I was invited to present it at open science events in Brazil, Chile, China and the USA.
