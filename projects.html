AI agents / Open source

diar.ia.br Studio

My role

Founder and editor of diar.ia.br, a weekday AI newsletter in Portuguese. I designed and built the pipeline that produces it.

Product

A public production pipeline on Claude Code that takes each edition from research to scheduling in six stages, with human review at two of them.

How it works

40 specialized AI agents and 44 skills, integrated with 20+ external APIs and 8 MCP servers. Built in TypeScript on Node, with typecheck, unit tests and an end-to-end smoke test on every pull request.

Outcomes

Automated the work previously done by a team member. The newsletter reaches 150,000+ people a month across its own audience and partner audiences.

Geojournalism / Open-source platform

Jeo

My role

InfoAmazonia already existed when I joined: I was hired as a consultant to diagnose its platform and propose improvements. From that work I conceived Jeo, made the case for building it, and led its development, later heading product and technology with a team of designers, engineers and data analysts.

Product

An open-source geojournalism platform for WordPress: news stories geo-referenced on maps. It grew out of the system behind InfoAmazonia, which aggregates news in Portuguese, Spanish and English from partners and the public.

Evolution

Before, InfoAmazonia ran on a manual Jekyll-based system that generated static content published straight to a Git repository, without even a search box. With no budget to maintain a proprietary platform long term, I proposed an open-source WordPress theme and a community around it. That became Jeo.

Outcomes

InfoAmazonia's pageviews grew by up to 400% in one year. Jeo was adopted by newsrooms on three continents, including Oxpeckers (South Africa), Ekuatorial (Indonesia) and InfoNile (Nile Basin). It is still maintained as a WordPress plugin, and InfoAmazonia still runs on it.

Open hardware / IoT

Rede InfoAmazonia

My role

I conceived and led the project inside InfoAmazonia, coordinating hardware development and the funding strategy.

Product

An open-source IoT platform for real-time water monitoring in the Amazon: low-cost sensors publishing open data about the region's rivers.

Evolution

One of the 10 projects funded among 800+ applicants to Google Impact Challenge Brazil 2014. The open approach inspired a group in China to build their own version of the system, which took me there to present the project.

Outcomes

The Google funding sustained the project for two years. The work involved six universities and led to a master's dissertation and a spin-off product.
