About me

I create award-winning products by alternating between business strategy and hands-on work.

As a child, I opened an Atari cartridge and, with the help of some cables, plugged it straight into the power socket. The cartridge broke and my home's power went out. It is my first memory of failure. Many more came later, and they guide my successes.

I carry that curiosity and daring with me. It is what led me to create Jandig, an open-source augmented reality platform awarded by Mozilla; Rede InfoAmazonia, an open-source IoT platform for water monitoring funded by a Google award; and Jeo, a geojournalism platform adopted by newsrooms on three continents. Their stories are in Projects.

Since then I have taken that way of working to larger companies: an AI product for industrial maintenance at Stefanini, an API product at Amazon, and a fee system and a partner API at AutoNation. Today I run diar.ia.br, a weekday AI newsletter produced by a pipeline of AI agents that I built.

Two decades managing projects taught me some valuable lessons. With my mind constantly bubbling with ideas, prioritization is my mantra. I have lost count of how many prototypes I have built. I do it for pleasure, and I am not attached to ideas that do not prove useful. Working with developer communities, I learned that the best way to manage is to understand people's motivations and encourage them, and that the best way to lead is by example.

How I learned my skills

Ideation

As an artist, my focus is to create a feeling in a specific context, and there are countless ways to do that. I learned ideation techniques to make interactive digital artworks possible: prototyping, Five Whys analysis, storyboarding, Crazy 8s.

Prioritization

This goes back more than a decade, when I led a small team of very creative people. Everyone had lots of ideas, and we had to decide together what to build with limited resources. A tool I like is the Value vs. Effort matrix: simple to understand, and it forces you to think about the two variables that matter most, outcomes and resources.

Communication

I love to teach. Adding up all the talks and workshops I have given, the number goes past 150. Teaching requires empathy with the audience: a time-constrained context where you cannot assume people already know something. I apply the same method whenever I need to communicate with anyone.

Stakeholder management

I worked on a Brazilian government project with more than 10 funders, some of whom we partnered with daily. At the same time, as Technology Coordinator, I had to synchronize my team's work with other teams' to maximize outcomes.

Leadership

My work as a community manager in open-source projects is based on "selling" ideas so that people invest their time in them, for free. To make that happen, you need to share a vision of what you want to build and lead people in building it.
